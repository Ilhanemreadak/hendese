Intro

Hendese · Motion

Sticky narrativeSticky figure, scrolling text.

The figure column sticks to the screen while the text column flows. Each [data-beat] becomes active when it crosses the reading line (45% of the screen), and the figure moves to that step's state. With motion off, the figure sits above the text in its finished state.

Classes
.sticky · .sticky-fig · .sf-head · .sticky-text · [data-beat] · .sticky--dense
JS
Hendese.stickyScene(cfg); render(i, t, api)
State
.on (active) and .past (passed) on beats

01

Anatomy

Two columns: the sticky figure on the left, the text being read on the right. Unlike the pin scene, progress comes from the text itself, not a track.

In live mode, .sticky turns into a two-column grid; .sticky-fig sticks at 100vh tall, and each [data-beat] inside .sticky-text takes at least 30vh. On every frame the engine finds the last beat that crossed the reading line (45% of the screen): i is its index, t the progress until the next beat (0..1). The active beat gets .on, the earlier ones .past.

Pin or sticky

If the text is long or there are several paragraphs per beat, use sticky; if the figure is the main narrator and the text is a few lines, use pin. With sticky, readers read at their own pace and the figure follows them.

02

Live example · canary release

The new version is first tried with a small share of traffic; if the metrics are clean, the share grows. Four beats, four traffic ratios.

02 · Fig. 1

The traffic share moves from the stable version to the canary

Load balancer

v1 · stable100%95%50%0% shut down

v2 · canary0%5%50%100% healthy

canary share

Fig. 1. The line weight and the bar below show the share of traffic going to the canary. The dashed box is the version that was shut down.

  1. Bring up the canary

    v2 starts as a single replica and passes its health check. It takes no traffic yet; the stable version keeps serving every request.

  2. Give it 5% of traffic

    The load balancer sends one request in twenty to the canary. For fifteen minutes, the error rate and latency are compared against the stable version.

  3. Raise the share to half

    If the metrics stay within thresholds, the share goes up to 50%. If a threshold is crossed, rolling back is one step: drop the share to zero.

  4. Hand over everything

    The canary takes all traffic and v1 is shut down. The release is done; Hoca's speech bubble says so too: traffic is 100% on the canary.

03

Variation · rich beats

A beat can be more than a paragraph: a table, a warning, code. For a dense drawing, .sticky--dense widens the figure column on narrow desktops; t gives the progress inside a beat.

Restoring from a backup takes four steps. In the third step, the log replay is drawn with t: as the reader moves through the beat, the green bar fills. Tables in the text column turn into row cards in live mode instead of scrolling sideways.

Pick the restore point

Open a new instance beside it

A new instance named orders-db-restore is opened from the 12:00 snapshot. The broken database stays in place; it may be needed for the investigation.

Replay the logs up to 14:05

The transaction logs after 12:00 are applied in order and stopped at exactly 14:05. Depending on data size, this step can take minutes.

Verify, then switch the traffic

Row counts and the last order number are compared with the expected values. If they match, the app's connection address is switched to the new instance.

Lost window

Writes from the two minutes between 14:05 and 14:07 aren't in the new instance; extract them separately from the application log.

04

With motion off

Reduced motion, below 1100px, print and no JS: the figure sits above the text in its finished state, and the beats sit below it as a normal list.

With live mode off, .sticky is a single column and nothing sticks. The figure shows the last beat's state: in the canary example above, traffic is 100% on the canary and v1 is shut down; in the restore example, the new instance is verified. The beats are fully opaque and have no .on / .past class. That is why the information of the in-between steps must be written in the text; the figure shows only the final state.

With reduced motion on desktop, Hoca sits inside the drawing as a still copy in every scene that has still. When the engine leaves live mode, it clears whatever was written with api.cls and api.set by itself.

05

Do / don't

The figure follows the text; the text explains the figure. Each must make sense on its own.

Do · the beat text states the state

  1. Give it 5% of traffic

    The load balancer sends one request in twenty to the canary.

Don't · information only in the figure

  1. Step two

    See the figure.

Do · one state change per beat

Four beats, four traffic ratios. Every change in the figure maps to exactly one beat; the reader always knows what changed.

Don't · many changes inside one beat

If three boxes appear and two lines vanish in a single beat, the reader can't tell which to follow. A single continuous change driven by t (like a bar filling) is enough.

Beat height

In live mode each beat is at least 30vh; even if the text is short, there is breathing room between steps. Don't pad beats with artificial space.

06

API

Register between Hendese.init() and Hendese.start(). If the selector isn't found, stickyScene returns null.

MemberWhat it does
elThe .sticky selector; contains .sticky-fig > .drawing and [data-beat] elements.
render(i, t, api)i is the active beat (from 0), t the progress inside the beat, 0..1. Returns {hud?, tb?, hoca?}; tb 0..1 turns the readout into a title block (.hud-tb).
chap, still, measure(api), reset(api)As in the pin scene.
apiel, fig, beats, drawing, aw, ah (aw/ah are read from the computed --aw/--ah on every measure; they change in the narrow variant), set(k, v) (writes --k to .sticky-fig), cls, live, poke, seg, ease, lerp, clamp01.
.on / .pastClasses the engine itself gives the beats; removed when live mode turns off.
.sticky--denseWidens the figure column and tightens the padding at 1100–1279px.
.hud-slotOptional; goes inside .sf-head, and the HUD lands there at 232px wide.
