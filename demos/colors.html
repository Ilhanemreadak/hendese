ColorsTwo themes, one line.

Every color is defined once in tokens.css with light-dark(light, dark). Components never write colors; they only read tokens, so adapting the theme comes down to overriding a few variables.

Tokens
--bg · --surface* · --paper · --scene-bg --scene-shade · --ink* · --accent* · --draw · --pencil* · --ok --warn --bad --sec (+ -soft) · --code-*
Theme
html[data-theme="light|dark"] · .scene-dark · .scene-light
JS
Not needed; the [data-theme-toggle] theme button works on its own

Figure 1. The top and right faces are --paper, the left face --scene-shade. In 0.2 the dark --paper was lightened to #1a2331; the old #141b25 could not be told apart from the sheet background (#121a27).

The rule is simple: --ink for everything that must be read, --ink-2 for the sentence that explains it, --ink-3 only for labels and metadata. Setting a paragraph in --ink-3 does not make it "minor"; it makes it "unreadable".

--scene-shade is a mix, not a color: 8% of --draw blended into the sheet background (.fill-shade). .scene-dark and .scene-light give the scene background automatically to a container that has no .frame-bg of its own.

--ok-soft, --warn-soft, --bad-soft and --sec-soft are the main color at 9–13% opacity. They go only under text of the same color, as a background; on their own they carry no color information.

A component reads its status color from --c and --c-soft; .s-ok, .s-warn, .s-bad, .s-sec, .s-neutral and .s-accent set both. Old names like .pill-ok, .state.ok and .readout.ok work until 1.0.

Why purple is separate

--sec is not an error or a warning: it is for permissions, secrets and access notes. Showing an access denial in purple rather than red tells the reader "permission missing", not "system broken".

Service colors are bound to the --c variable through the .s-aws, .s-gitlab, .s-k8s and .s-argo classes; on stations, .svc-* does the same job. If your diagram makes no distinction between services, ignore these four.

05

Theme mechanism

Tokens are resolved with light-dark(); the element's color-scheme decides which side is picked. That is why forcing a theme and changing the accent color each take one line.

light-dark() looks at the element's own color-scheme. So giving a container color-scheme:dark (which is exactly what .scene-dark is) resolves every token below it as dark. The container must read its own background and color again; an inherited color arrives already resolved on the parent.

To force the whole page, <html data-theme="dark"> is enough. Theme buttons ([data-theme-toggle]) change this attribute, store the choice in localStorage under the hendese-theme key and dispatch the hendese:theme event. To avoid a theme flash on load, the dist/head.js line is inlined in the <head>.

Overriding tokens

The whole theme lives inside @layer hendese.*. Any rule you write unlayered wins regardless of specificity; no !important or long selectors needed. Still give both themes in one line.

The theme changes with a single color. --draw, --accent-soft, --pencil, --pencil-2 and --scene-shade are derived from --accent with color-mix(); overriding --accent on :root carries all of them. --accent-2 is the link text and is set separately for contrast.

Overriding on a descendant

The derived tokens are resolved once on :root and inherited as is. If you override only --accent on one box, the section number and focus ring change but the lines keep the old color. In a local override, write the derived tokens too; the box below does that.

Layer order

If you put your overrides in your own @layer, layer order comes into play: a layer declared before the Hendese layers loses. The safest choice is to write them unlayered.

06

Contrast

Text and status tokens are tuned to pass the WCAG AA threshold (4.5:1) in both themes. There are two known exceptions, both on the same background.

Exception

Do not put --ok or --warn colored text on the darkest surface (--surface-3); that is also the inline code background. Status text belongs on --surface, --paper or its own -soft background.

For a color-blind reader the three squares on the right look the same; for a screen reader they do not exist. Status is always given with a word (.state, .pill) or a labeled icon.

High contrast preference

If "increase contrast" is on in the operating system (prefers-contrast: more), four tokens are redefined: pencil lines turn into ink, and the visible border and secondary text darken one step. Components read tokens, so no other rule is needed. The box on the right below sets the same four values locally, so you see the difference even with the setting off.

Once pencil turns into ink, the difference between solid and dashed survives only in the line pattern, not in color. That is why "planned" is always drawn dashed; a faded color alone is not enough.

Forced colors

In Windows high contrast mode (forced-colors: active) the browser maps backgrounds and colors to the system palette; meaning conveyed only by a background is lost. The package draws selected and current states (the .choice selection, a pressed .toggle-btn, the selected station, aria-current in .steps) with a frame in the Highlight color, and progress bars and a checked checkbox with a Highlight background; disabled elements get GrayText. In your own components, convey meaning with a border, a pattern or text too.
